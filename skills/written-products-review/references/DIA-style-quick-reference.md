# DIA Style Quick Reference

> Distilled from DIA-style-manual.md for fast lookup. For edge cases, consult the full manual.

## Words of Estimative Probability (WEPs)

| Phrase | Probability |
|--------|-------------|
| Almost certainly / Almost certainly not | >95% / <5% |
| Very likely / Very unlikely | 85–95% / 5–15% |
| Likely / Unlikely | 55–85% / 15–45% |
| Roughly even chance | 45–55% |
| Possibly / We cannot rule out | <45%, lower confidence |

Confidence syntax: *(High confidence)* / *(Moderate confidence)* / *(Low confidence)*
Low confidence requires explicit explanation: "*(Low confidence — limited sourcing; no committee testimony on record)*"

## Analytic Phrase Map

| Judgment type | Required phrase |
|---------------|-----------------|
| Analytic judgment | "We assess…" |
| Quantitative forecast | "We estimate…" |
| Low-confidence possibility | "We cannot rule out…" |
| Recommendation | "We recommend considering…" |
| Significant uncertainty | "We judge with low confidence that…" |

Never: "we believe" → use "we assess"

---

## Banned Qualifier Stacking

Never combine a judgment verb (*indicate, imply, suggest*) with a qualifier (*may, likely, probably*):

- ❌ "The lull **may indicate** rebels are ready to negotiate."
- ✅ "The lull **indicates** rebels are ready to negotiate." (direct)
- ✅ "The lull **may mean** rebels are ready to negotiate." (qualified)

---

## could / may / might

- *could* = capability only ("The missile **could** reach the target" = it is within range)
- *may / might* = judgment or prediction ("The missile **may** be targeted…")
- Never stack with *possibly* or *conceivably*

---

## Buried Verbs — Cut Every Time

| Weak | Strong |
|------|--------|
| made a commitment to resume | committed to resuming |
| in violation of the treaty | violates the treaty |
| conducted an inspection of | inspected |
| executed an attack on | attacked |

---

## Banned Phrases

- "it could be argued" → say who argues it, or cut
- "it is believed" → attributed by whom? Cut or attribute
- "some feel / some suggest / some say" → quantify or attribute
- "it appears / it seems" → replace with confidence-level statement
- "obviously / clearly / certainly" without sourcing → cut
- "we believe" → use "we assess"
- "it is, there are, there were, there will be" as sentence openers → rewrite
- "it is likely" (passive) → "We assess this is likely"

## Sentence Structure

- Maximum 20 words per sentence
- Active voice: "The committee advanced the bill" — not "The bill was advanced"
- BLUF first: lead with the bottom-line assessment
- Paragraph structure: claim → evidence → analysis

---

## Numbers (Key Rules)

- 10 or greater: use numerals
- Less than 10: spell out (except measurements, time, money)
- Mixed in same sentence: use numerals for all
- Percentages: always numerals, spell out "percent" in text
- Dates: military format in products (15 August 1947), traditional in correspondence

---

## Abbreviations

- Introduce on first use: "motorized rifle division (MRD)"
- Avoid in key judgments, executive summaries, or titles
- Do not use person's name abbreviated (Kim, not KJU)
- Plural: add lowercase s (MRDs), no apostrophe

---

## Capitalization — Common Intelligence Writing Cases

- Proper names of military forces: capitalize full + shortened form ("the U.S. Army; the Army")
- Generic references: lowercase ("naval vessels"; "army equipment")
- Government bodies: capitalize proper name + shortened form ("the U.S. Congress; the Congress")
- Political parties: capitalize full + shortened ("the Chinese Communist Party; the CCP; the party")
- Titles before name: capitalize ("Prime Minister Cameron")
- Titles after or replacing name: lowercase ("the prime minister")

---

## Compounding Reference

For specific compound word spellings (hyphenated, solid, or separate), consult the full word list in DIA-style-manual.md (Chapter 7). It has 10,000+ entries.

Common rules:
- Unit modifiers before noun: hyphenate ("fire-resistant material")
- After noun: no hyphen ("the material was fire resistant")
- Adverbs ending in -ly: no hyphen ("recently designed building")
- Prefixes to non-capitalized words: solid ("counterintelligence," "multinational")
- Prefixes to capitalized words: hyphen ("anti-Castro," "non-U.S.")
