# P2: First read — highlights only

*Derived from the checklist's "Quietly read the product through once."*

Read the whole product through once, in **VIEWING mode**. No judgment. No edits. No findings. This pass exists to do one thing: notice what is **great**.

This matters for two reasons. First, it forces you to encounter the document as its reader will — whole, before you start pulling it apart. Second, the **highlights** you produce here are the baseline the **fresh-eyes** sub-agent is compared against in Stage 6: if the cold reader's takeaways don't match what you flagged as great, the document isn't landing the way the author intends.

## What to do

1. Read the entire document once, start to finish, at reading pace.
2. Note every element that is genuinely strong — a sharp insight, a clean argument, an elegant piece of structure, a line that lands, a chart that earns its space.
3. Do **not** record problems. If you notice a flaw, let it go — it will be caught in the stage that owns it. Recording problems here contaminates the viewing pass.

## Stage output — Highlights

```
## Highlights (P2)

- [what was great, and where — be specific enough that the author knows exactly what you mean]
- …

**One-line take:** [if you had to tell a colleague the single best thing about this product, what is it?]
```

Be specific and be honest. Empty praise ("nice job!") is useless as a Stage 6 baseline. "The three-tier framing in §2 makes the trade-off legible in a way the raw numbers never could" is a real highlight.

## Completion gate

The gate passes when:

1. The whole document has been read once in viewing mode.
2. The **highlights** list is produced, specific, and free of any problem-flagging.
3. A one-line take is recorded.

The highlights persist for the whole session. Present them, then ask the author: proceed to Stage 1 (Analysis), or resubmit.
