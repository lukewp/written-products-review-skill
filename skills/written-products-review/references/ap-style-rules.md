# Style rules — AP *(Associated Press)*

*Loaded at Stage 3 when the brief's style standard is **AP**. For journalism, press releases, media advisories, and public communications. Based on the Associated Press Stylebook (2024–2026, 57th ed.).*

The base checklist rules in `03-line-edit.md` apply, **with the AP overrides below**. When an AP rule overrides a base checklist rule, state it explicitly in the Stage 3 output (e.g., "AP omits the Oxford comma — the base checklist requires it; AP wins here unless you override").

## Overrides of the base checklist

- **No Oxford comma** by default. "red, white and blue" — not "red, white, and blue". *This is the headline AP override; the base checklist mandates the Oxford comma, so flag it.* The exception: keep the serial comma where its absence creates ambiguity.
- **Sentence length: 25 words** (same cap as General). AP prizes short sentences and short paragraphs.

## Numbers

- **Spell out one through nine; use figures for 10 and up.** Exceptions: ages, percentages, money, dimensions, and dates always take figures ("5 years old", "3%", "$4", "7 a.m.", "June 5").
- **Never start a sentence with a figure** — spell it out or recast the sentence. (Exception: calendar years.)
- **Percent:** use the % sign with a figure ("60%") in AP's current guidance.
- **Millions/billions:** "$4 million", "7 billion people".

## Titles

- **Capitalize a formal title only directly before a name:** "President Jane Doe", but "Jane Doe, the president,". Lowercase titles standing alone or set off by commas.
- **Courtesy titles** (Mr./Ms./Dr.) generally dropped after first reference; use last name alone.

## Dates, times, places

- **Abbreviate months** with specific dates: "Jan. 5", but spell out standalone months ("in January"). March, April, May, June, July are never abbreviated.
- **Times:** figures + "a.m."/"p.m."; "noon" and "midnight", not "12 p.m."
- **States:** AP abbreviations in datelines; spell out in body text per current AP guidance.

## Attribution and voice

- **"said" is the default attribution verb** — neutral, invisible. Avoid "claimed", "asserted", "argued" unless you intend their coloring.
- **Attribute opinion and contested fact.** In AP-style work, sourcing is explicit: who says so?
- Otherwise apply the General standard's guidance on strong verbs, cut hedges, and active voice — AP and Smart Brevity agree on tightness.
