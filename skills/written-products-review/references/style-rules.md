# Style rules — General *(default standard)*

*Loaded at Stage 3 when the brief's style standard is **General**. Derived from the checklist's line-by-line rules and its cited sources: Smart Brevity (Allen, Schwartz & VandeHei), The Elements of Style (Strunk & White), Line by Line (Cook), Politics and the English Language (Orwell), and The Visual Display of Quantitative Information (Tufte).*

The base checklist rules in `03-line-edit.md` always apply. This file adds the General standard's specifics and is the ruleset a **user overlay** overrides.

## Sentence and word

- **Sentence length: 25 words maximum.** Flag every sentence over the cap (current/potential).
- **One idea per sentence.** If a sentence carries two ideas joined by "and" or a semicolon, consider splitting.
- **Cut "that" and "which"** except where the sentence can't be rewritten more succinctly without them.
- **Three words → one.** "In order to" → "to". "Due to the fact that" → "because". "At this point in time" → "now".
- **Cut extra syllables.** Prefer the shorter word carrying the same meaning: "use" over "utilize", "help" over "facilitate", "about" over "approximately" (unless precision is the point).
- **Active voice by default.** Passive is a deliberate choice (to foreground the object or when the actor is unknown), not a default. Flag buried actors: "a decision was made" → "the board decided".
- **Strong verbs, not buried ones.** "made a decision" → "decided". "conducted an analysis of" → "analyzed". "is reflective of" → "reflects".
- **Cut hedges and intensifiers.** "very", "really", "quite", "somewhat", "it is important to note that", "arguably". Strip the adjective; keep the claim.
- **Concrete over abstract.** Prefer the specific noun to the category. "the vehicles" beats "the assets" if you mean trucks.

## Emphasis and layout

- **Selective bolding.** Bold key ideas — a few per page. Bolding everything bolds nothing.
- **White space** makes key findings stand out; let them breathe.
- **Headline over "Executive Summary".** Consider a brief summary headline stating the finding instead of the generic label; if used, the first sentence gives detail under it.
- **Oxford comma:** use it. (Under the General standard the base checklist rule stands — this is where AP differs.)
- **Emoji** for emphasis only if the audience in the brief warrants it.

## Tufte, for anything with data

- Maximize the data-ink ratio: cut chartjunk, gridlines, and decoration that carry no information.
- Every chart earns its space by making a comparison the prose can't. A chart that only restates one number is a sentence.
- Label directly; make the reader's eye travel as little as possible.

## Orwell's six rules (the backstop)

1. Never use a metaphor you're used to seeing in print.
2. Never use a long word where a short one will do.
3. If it's possible to cut a word, cut it.
4. Never use the passive where you can use the active.
5. Never use a foreign phrase, a scientific word, or a jargon word if an everyday English equivalent exists.
6. Break any of these rules sooner than say anything outright barbarous.
