# Review 2: Organization

*Derived from the checklist's "Second Review: Organization." Assess against the **brief**.*

Analysis that's sound but badly ordered still fails the reader. This stage checks whether the most important, useful, and actionable material reaches the reader first, and whether the body is logically built.

## Completion criteria — assess each

- [ ] **Executive summary leads with what matters.** Does the Executive Summary present the most important, useful, or actionable points **first**?
- [ ] **Short, not shallow.** Is the Executive Summary free of needlessly repetitive or extraneous content — brief without being thin?
- [ ] **Body is logically organized.** Does the structure follow a defensible logic?
- [ ] **Five-section contemplation.** Consider whether a five-section structure would serve this product better than its current one:
  1. Executive Summary / Big Idea (brief)
  2. What's Next (brief)
  3. Bottom Line — why it matters (brief)
  4. By The Numbers (detail)
  5. The Big Picture (detailed analysis)

  This is a *contemplation*, not a mandate — assess whether the current structure delivers the same job and note the gap if it doesn't.
- [ ] **Assert–support–analyze.** In prose sections, does the report follow an **assert → support → analyze** flow — conclusions, factual support, and analysis organized around intro sentences with relevant transitions? Watch for the **analyze** leg collapsing: a report that asserts and cites but never explains, in its own words, *why the evidence matters and how it advances the argument*, has a missing leg.
- [ ] **Self-contained.** Does the report avoid requiring the reader to bring external information to understand it?
- [ ] **Praise the structure.** Are there great arguments or clean analyses worth flagging for appreciation?

## Completion gate

Passes when every item is assessed, the five-section contemplation is done explicitly, findings are actionable, and the author confirms: proceed to Stage 3, or resubmit.
