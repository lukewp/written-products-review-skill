# Insights Report Checklist

## DRAFT v6.0 \--3/05/2025 by Luke Peterson 

# Before reviewing, learn:

*(All these things should be in the Project/Epic description)*  
\[ \] Who is the intended reader?  
\[ \] What decisions might they be confronting?  
\[ \] What is their mission, objective, and approach?  
\[ \] What is their span of action and influence?  
\[ \] What specific questions did they ask us to address?  
\[ \] What is an update or other topic you’re familiar with and they need to know about?  
\[ \] Why did we choose to prioritize this work above other work?  
\[ \] Why will the reader find this work significant?

# Quietly read the product through once

\[ \] VIEWING mode. No judgment or edits on first pass; highlight things you think were great\!

# First Review: Analysis and Strategic Insight

\[ \] Does the presented analysis directly answer the specific questions asked?  
\[ \] Does the report contain insights that directly advise the reader’s decision on an action or point of influence within their span of control?  
\[ \] Does all analysis avoid reliance on unstated assumptions?  
\[ \] Does the report assert the basis of its findings? (e.g. Methodology)  
\[ \] We are confident in our expertise or we have consulted appropriate subject matter experts to confirm observations & insights  
\[ \] Do all of the report’s conclusions that are in conflict with one another have a clear explanation?  
\[ \] If the product has a similar topic to other recent internally-written products, does it refer to findings from those other products?   
\[ \] If the report is intended to communicate one big idea, does it do so cleanly? Is it free from extraneous/ exhaustive findings that could be better presented in a separate product?  
\[ \] If its findings conflict with conclusions previously presented, do we explain why?  
\[ \] If its findings conflict with other external prominent research or commonly-held beliefs, do we explain why?  
\[ \] Does the analysis avoid leaving major unanswered questions?  
\[ \] Are there any very insightful elements that you could compliment the authors on?

# Second Review: Organization

\[ \] Does the Executive Summary present the most important, useful, or actionable points first?  
\[ \] Is the Executive Summary free from needlessly repetitive or extraneous content? (“short not shallow”)  
\[ \] Is the body of the report logically organized?   
	\[ \] Contemplate a five-section structure:   
1\. Executive Summary/Big Idea (brief);   
2\. What’s Next (brief);   
3\. Bottom Line (brief why-it-matters);   
4\. By The Numbers (detail);   
5\. The Big Picture (detailed analysis)  
\[ \] In parts of the product following a prose format with paragraphs or sections, does the report follow an “assert” \- “support” \- “analyze” format in which conclusions, analysis, and factual support statements are organized around intro sentences with relevant transitions?  
\[ \] Does the report avoid requiring the reader to have any external information to understand the report?  
\[ \] Are there any great arguments or clean analyses you could flag for appreciation?

# Third Review: Line-by-Line

\[ \] Does the product align to the relevant Style Guide?  
\[ \] All sentences have maximum 25 words.  
\[ \] The report does not contain words “that” and “which” except where language can’t be rewritten more succinctly without them.  
\[ \] The authors eliminated three words where two or one works.  
\[ \] Authors cut extra syllables.  
\[ \] Authors selectively bolded key ideas.  
\[ \] Authors used white space to make key findings stand out.  
\[ \] Authors considered a brief summary headline instead of the words “Executive Summary” as header.  
\[ \] If employing this approach, the first sentence should offer detail under the headline.  
\[ \] If appropriate for intended audience, use emoji for emphasis (e.g., in section headers).  
\[ \] Authors use the Oxford comma unless strenuously instructed by management not to do so.

# Fourth Review: Read out loud

\[ \] You have wiped prior reviews of this product from your mind and have read out loud to yourself.  
\[ \] It sounds conversational: you’ve flagged awkward language for clarity and none remain.  
\[ \] You’ve read the paper backwards to catch typos.  
\[ \] You’ve critically reviewed the six-words-or-less title and it makes sense? What about the document title (filename)?

# Fifth Review: Sensitivity Check

\[ \] The report contains nothing that would be scandalous if a newspaper said “In its official research, Entity ABC determines XYZ”? (if it does contain this type of information, we are rock-solid in the assertion? If we are not rock-solid, we have tempered the assertion to as accurate as possible and explicitly given leadership a heads up?)  
\[ \] The product contains no assertions colleagues or clients may consider embarrassing, that would warrant a private briefing to them before releasing the doc more broadly.   
\[ \] (Or, we have already given such a briefing to all relevant parties)  
\[ \] The product is appropriately marked based on its direct derivative citations of sensitive or classified materials.

# Sixth Review: Sanity Check

\[ \] Have somebody who has not read this report yet do a basic scan for errors.

# Seventh Review: Packaging

\[ \] All fonts used are generally-available ([email/web-safe fonts list](https://knowledgebase.constantcontact.com/email-digital-marketing/articles/KnowledgeBase/5877-web-safe-font-selections-in-campaign-emails?lang=en_US))  
\[ \] Header and body fonts, if different, are consistent and intentional  
\[ \] Included images are presented at a resolution appropriate for layout, not pixelated  
\[ \] Embedded links are clickable, verified  
\[ \] Page numbers exist

# Eighth Review: Accessibility

\[ \] Alt text exists for all images.  
\[ \] Document section headers use a header tag rather than just a larger font.  
\[ \] Tables have a header tag on the header row elements.  
\[ \] Chart images are alt-tagged; title and description in-line in the document text and not in the image.

# Ninth Review: Presentation (if applicable)

\[ \] No font sizes smaller than 20 if intended to support speaker presentation  
\[ \] “Breathing Space” for margin notes exists on all slides not intended to be full-bleed  
\[ \] Ruler, guides used to ensure consistent positioning of recurring design elements like headers  
\[ \] Delivered in a PDF format instead of a PPTX; final copy compared side-by-side to validate look-and-feel

# Tenth Review: Publishing (if applicable)

*This section ensures a document published for public consumption online is well-positioned to be cited in AI prompt responses and identified in search.*

\[ \] Keywords in URL slug show LLMs/engines the page is worth parsing for relevant prompts/searches  
\[ \] Assess cited sources from relevant LLM prompt responses: ensuring this document shares characteristics with content cited in answers to relevant prompts (e.g., definitions page, clear purpose paragraph, uses specific terms of art as applicable) can amplify its likelihood of citation in future prompts  
\[ \] If the document mentions one brand or competitor, consider also mentioning other brands/competitors on a pros/cons grid in order for AI to score it as an objective information source.  
\[ \] Eliminate subjective, loaded, emotional, speculative language to raise AI likelihood of citation: Write what you can prove, not what you feel. The reader should draw conclusions from the facts—not be led there by your adjectives. Keep it tight, keep it neutral, and trust that the truth is compelling enough on its own.
